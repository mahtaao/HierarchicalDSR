METAMeta-reviewstructural argument + synthetic benchmark; five points to address▶

Summary

Argues that latent-source identifiability claims based only on observational scalp EEG/MEG are not empirically falsifiable, and should require either synthetic evaluation with known ground truth or validation in intervention-capable settings. Supported by a structural argument about the scalp inverse problem and a synthetic benchmark comparing PCA, FastICA, TCL, iVAE across five stress axes.

Strengths

  • Clear, relevant position on the gap between theoretical identifiability and empirical biological source recovery.
  • The five stress axes usefully connect method assumptions to EEG-specific failure conditions.
  • The distinction between useful representations and identified biological sources is important.

Weaknesses

  • Does not always separate mathematical identifiability, whether assumptions hold on real EEG, and whether recovered components correspond to biological sources. Strongest claims should be scoped to empirical biological source recovery.
  • Unclear how widespread the criticized practice is — few concrete examples of scalp-level studies making identifiability claims without validation.
  • Empirical clarifications needed: generator described with four latent dims while the source-count experiment varies up to 22; the central M≫D regime is not tested; the "every method drops from near-perfect to near-chance" claim is inconsistent with results.
  • TCL is load-bearing but never succeeds even under favorable conditions. Implementation differs from the original, uses only eight segments, and it's unclear whether the required post-processing ICA step was applied. Without a positive control, results may reflect implementation failure.
  • Conclusions may depend strongly on the generator and stress-test construction. The learned generator is not biophysically validated, and the source-coupling experiment may amount to additional linear mixing rather than dependent source dynamics.

Requested in rebuttal

(1) Precisely define which identifiability claim applies and separate theorem validity from empirical/biological interpretation. (2) Concrete evidence the claims appear in current scalp EEG literature. (3) Clarify the TCL pipeline and show it succeeds under at least one assumption-satisfying condition. (4) Resolve the latent-dimension and M vs. D inconsistencies and correct claims not supported by Fig 2. (5) Explain robustness to the generator, preprocessing choices, and stress-axis construction.

Summary

The central claim appears to be a common-sense consequence of applying latent-variable models to brain data: model-estimated latent variables cannot be directly verified as true biological variables.

Strengths

Addresses an important topic at the intersection of causal representation learning and neuroscience. The discussion of validating latent-variable models on observational brain data is reasonable. Clearly written and well organized.

Weaknesses

The limitation is well known: model-estimated latents can't be directly verified as biological. Unclear what new insight the paper adds. Practical relevance of identifiability for neuroscience is unclear beyond theory. ICA's guarantees rely on strong assumptions unlikely to hold in the brain — the difficulty of verifying recovered latents reflects model-assumption limitations. The authors appear to elevate a well-known limitation into the central contribution.

Questions

  1. What is the new methodological insight beyond reiterating this general observation?
  2. How should conclusions be interpreted beyond demonstrating violations of the model assumptions?
  3. The benchmark is generated by another latent-variable model, so evaluation is model-dependent. How can robustness to the choice of generative model be demonstrated?

Summary

Argues latent-source identifiability claims for nonlinear-ICA and CRL methods are unfalsifiable on observational scalp EEG/MEG: the inverse problem is underdetermined; evaluation lacks ground truth; nonstationarity-exploiting methods are biased toward nuisance confounders (1/f noise) rather than cognitive sources. Builds a synthetic testbed by training a shPLRNN on real EEG and using recurrent states as ground-truth latents. Four methods across five axes; TCL and iVAE collapse to near-chance. On real EEG, TCL fails to align with MNE ground-truth, likely latching onto 1/f noise.

Strengths

  1. Addresses a critical issue — unfalsifiability and method-dependence of identifiability claims — justified by three lines of reasoning.
  2. The shPLRNN generator with ground-truth access is a novel benchmark filling a real gap.
  3. Robust evaluation across axes; real-EEG results give strong evidence (TCL captures 1/f noise).
  4. Proposes new evaluation standards.

Weaknesses

  1. §4 states a fixed latent dimension (L168); §5 sweeps up to 22 (L226).
  2. Core issue is the underdetermined case where sources far exceed channels, but experiments strictly test the opposite; the critical regime is never evaluated.
  3. Fig 2 caption ("every method drops from near-perfect to near-chance") is contradicted: FastICA stays robust and never drops; TCL is at chance throughout and never near-perfect.
  4. Ignores standard EEG cleaning (RANSAC, ICA-based artifact removal) routinely applied before analysis.

Questions

  1. Was the generator retrained for each "sources vs. channels" variation?
  2. Would the untested regime governing real scalp EEG matter for evaluating one of the named methods?
  3. If standard preprocessing were applied, would the §3 step-2 claims still hold?
4xW1oBorderline reject · conf 4 · thoroughness 4▶
Support 2Significance 2Presentation 3Context 3Discussion 3Agreement 4

Summary

Argues EEG latent-source estimation methods need validation via synthetic data and interventional evidence, and otherwise remain unfalsifiable. Methods evaluated on synthetic data testing robustness to the breakdown of five hypotheses, plus a real-EEG case study.

Strengths

Clear problem, reasonable position, clear reasoning followed by clear synthetic evidence (except TCL). The argument that TCL's classifier may be more sensitive to large-scale drifts than cognition-relevant sources is reasonable and well-formulated. The irreducibly underdetermined inverse problem and absence of method-independent ground truth are structural properties of the modality.

Weaknesses

TCL is load-bearing but the implementation appears too weak to be representative. Vs. Hyvärinen & Morioka (2016): they Morlet-filtered around the alpha band and found performance rises with more segments. Here: a per-timestep MLP (3 layers, width 64) + multinomial logistic regression over 8 segments, no preprocessing mentioned. The original used up to 512 segments; 8 is the lowest, worst-performing setting. Dropout/normalization layers appear omitted. These mismatches could plausibly explain the low TCL performance across the board, including under favorable conditions — so the implementation doesn't fairly represent TCL, for both synthetic and real-EEG experiments.

Questions

Would benefit from clearer scoping — how widespread is the problem in practice? Some relevant literature already includes synthetic benchmarks. §2 could detail prevalence, or at least give examples of scalp-level-only publications making identifiability claims; is this specifically a TCL issue? Would it be possible to rerun TCL with band-filtered spectral features and a segment count in the original's demonstrated range (≥64, ideally swept)? A stronger TCL implementation could significantly strengthen the argument.

Writing the rebuttal box — rules & approach

NeurIPS 2026 author response. Post via the per-review Rebuttal buttons in OpenReview. Use the meta-review as your guide — it names what would most change the AC's view.

Length10,000 characters per review. Plain text with OpenReview markdown; no file uploads.
ScopeClarify reviewers'/ACs' questions. No paper or supplementary revisions — the original submission stays the basis for the decision. New results are allowed inline.
No linksNone anywhere. Only exception: if reviewers asked for code, send an anonymized link to the AC in an Official Comment (all linked files anonymized).
AnonymityMust not reveal author identity or otherwise break double-blind.
AudienceCheck that the readers of each message are the ones you intend before posting.
CadenceEngage early and often — reviewers may follow up in Phase 2. Don't wait for the last day. Keep it factual (Code of Conduct).
StyleDraft and edit every response with /mahta-writing — apply Mahta's academic writing style throughout.
Tone & framingBe respectful and attentive to each reviewer's response and their intention. Where they're right to criticize and we were sloppy — something needing substantial change — frame the fix so it doesn't damage our image: present it as an update for clarity / coherence, with a brief, solid reason for why the issue arose in the first place. Concede the point gracefully, then show the improvement.
